TEF — Section A: Smart Practice Order

Your task
  • Section A: 5 minutes
  • Ask your conversation partner around ten questions.
  • Your examiner will play the role of this person.
  • Objective: to gather specific information.
Smart practice order

This order is built for coverage-by-probability under a hard time budget: the first 21 items give you exactly one worked example of every response family plus every one-off ad, so even a learner who stops early has rehearsed all 21 distinct question-checklists at least once. Families are led by their cleanest, highest-transfer archetype and sequenced largest-first (Adventure & active leisure, then Courses, Tours, Events, Boating, Clubs…), so the question sets you reuse most are learned first. From rank 22 the families round-robin in size order, so bigger (more probable) families recur more often and new patterns interleave with spaced reviews of earlier ones; same-family items are generally kept several items apart rather than in long early blocks. Near-duplicate ads (a second ski school, a third licence-free boat rental, breakfast-vs-picnic delivery, flight-vs-agency) are deliberately deferred because once you've done the archetype their marginal value is near zero. The final 8 slots (ranks 71–78) are therefore pure near-duplicates — the safest items to skip if you run out of days — ending with #48, essentially the same ad as #50. Difficulty also ramps: clean, well-structured ads come first and sparser or trickier ones later. As a safeguard, an independent second analysis was run on the same items shown in reverse order (to strip out any ordering bias); it reproduced the same family structure and the same redundant, skippable tail, confirming this order.

Biggest families: Adventure & active leisure (17%), Courses & lessons (12%), Tours & guided visits (9%), then Events & parties, Clubs & associations, and Boating & water outings (6% each).

Each item shows its original number · groups of 5 = one day (5/day × 14 days). The last items (Optional) are the most redundant — skip these first if time runs short.
