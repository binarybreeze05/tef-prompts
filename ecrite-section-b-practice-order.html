TEF — Written Section B: Smart Practice Order

Your task
  • Section B: write a structured argumentative letter (~200 words).
  • Open with a salutation and state the claim you are responding to.
  • Give three developed arguments, then a conclusion.
  • Close with a formal sign-off. Use advanced linking words throughout.
Smart practice order

The 73 claims are sorted into 11 response families ranked by how often each topic recurs in this pool, which proxies exam probability. Round 1 (ranks 1–11) delivers one clean archetype per family, most-probable first, so even a single session covers every major letter-pattern; each archetype is chosen to exercise its family's full argument bank and vocabulary. Subsequent rounds round-robin the families in probability order, so the biggest topics (ecology, education, technology) recur most often and each family's members stay spaced roughly 11 items apart for interleaved spaced repetition. Difficulty ramps deliberately: the cleanest, highest-frequency claims lead for confidence, while hybrid and edge cases (e.g. pollution-fatalism #6, diplomas #20) fall later. The tail (ranks 65–73) holds low-transfer outliers first, then near-duplicate claims that elicit almost the same letter — the safe items to drop if the 14 days run short, with the verbatim vegetarian duplicate (#46) dead last. As a safeguard, an independent second analysis was run on the same items shown in reverse order (to strip out any ordering bias); it reproduced the same family structure and the same redundant, skippable tail, confirming this order.

Biggest families: Ecology & pollution (15%), Education & school (15%), Technology & social media (12%), Books/reading & writing (10%), Health & diet (10%) — these five cover ~62% of the pool.

Each item shows its original number · groups of 5 = one day (5/day × 14 days). The last items (Optional) are the most redundant — skip these first if time runs short.
