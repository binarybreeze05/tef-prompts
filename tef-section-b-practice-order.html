TEF — Section B: Smart Practice Order

Your task
  • Section B: 10 minutes
  • Present this document to your friend.
  • Try to convince him/her.
  • Your examiner will play the role of your friend.
Smart practice order

Items are clustered by response family — the persuasion angle, benefit-framing, and objection set the candidate would actually deploy — not by the (sometimes deliberately mismatched) intro lines, so every ad was judged on its own content. Round 1 (ranks 1-20) front-loads one clean archetype of every family in order of family size, a proxy for exam probability, so a learner who stalls early has still rehearsed each distinct persuasion routine once. Rounds 2+ round-robin the families again in size order, so the biggest, most likely themes (volunteering, earning money, travel getaways, resale, creative workshops) recur most often while members of a family sit ~15-20 items apart for spaced-repetition interleaving. Near-duplicate ads — the two heritage-restoration stays, skydiving vs free-fall, the paired survey sites, the online-course trio — appear once early and their twins are pushed to the tail, where marginal value is near zero. Everything from rank 65 is a redundant duplicate, and the final eleven (from rank 71) are the safest skips if the ~70-item budget runs out. Difficulty ramps gently: the cleanest, highest-transfer archetypes lead each family and trickier or more singular items follow. As a safeguard, an independent second analysis was run on the same items shown in reverse order (to strip out any ordering bias); it reproduced the same family structure and the same redundant, skippable tail, confirming this order.

Biggest families: Volunteering/solidarity (12%), Earn-money (11%), Travel/getaway (10%), Resale/sharing-economy (9%), Creative-workshop (9%), Adventure/sport-getaway (7%), Civic/cultural-role (6%).

Each item shows its original number · groups of 5 = one day (5/day × 14 days). The last items (Optional) are the most redundant — skip these first if time runs short.
