TEF — Written Section A: Smart Practice Order

Your task
  • Section A: write between 100 and 200 words (minimum 80).
  • Organise your response into 3 to 4 paragraphs.
  • Use a variety of tenses and forms (passé composé, imparfait, plus-que-parfait, passive, past subjunctive, past conditional).
  • Use advanced linking words for coherence (however, indeed, nevertheless, moreover…).
Smart practice order

The 72 openers were grouped into 18 response families defined by shared narrative scaffold, tense usage, and reusable vocabulary — not surface topic — so practising one member strongly prepares you for the rest. Round 1 (ranks 1–18) front-loads one clean archetype from every family, most-probable families first, so even if you stall early you have rehearsed each distinct pattern once. Rounds 2 onward round-robin the families in size order, taking each family's next-most-representative member, which spaces members roughly 18 apart for interleaving and makes the biggest families (discovery, rescue, crime) recur most, matching their exam probability. The tail (rank 66+) holds the least-representative members of saturated families and the five near-duplicate twins, with exact duplicates placed dead last as the safest items to skip. Early items deliberately teach the transferable templates — the "ordinary→discovery→reveal" arc, the rescue arc, comeuppance, and the "original solution" arc — that later items recycle. As a safeguard, an independent second analysis was run on the same items shown in reverse order (to strip out any ordering bias); it reproduced the same family structure and the same redundant, skippable tail, confirming this order.

Biggest families: Surprising discovery (12.5%), Rescue/hero (9.7%), Crime uncovered (8.3%), Travel mishap (8.3%), then Animal antics / Poetic justice / Quirky spectacle (6.9% each).

Each item shows its original number · groups of 5 = one day (5/day × 14 days). The last items (Optional) are the most redundant — skip these first if time runs short.
